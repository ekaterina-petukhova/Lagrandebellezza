La Grande Bellezza

Paolo Sorrentino

"I was looking for the great beauty, but I didn't find it. This is how it always ends. With death. But first, there was life. Hidden beneath the blah, blah, blah. It is all settled beneath the chitter chatter and the noise. Silence and sentiment. Emotion and fear. The haggard, inconstant flashes of beauty. And then the wretched squalor and miserable humanity. All buried under the cover of the embarrassment of being in the world."
Enter the great beauty ↓

Beauty on the surface

The Great Beauty is precisely that — beauty on the surface, artificial, put on display. Sorrentino isn't searching for meaning behind it; he revels in the surface itself: the glitter of the parties, the gloss of Rome, the flawlessness of emptiness. There is no depth here to be uncovered — only a shining shell, and in that shine lies everything. Beauty doesn't conceal meaning; it replaces it.

Beneath the shell

In The Great Beauty, Rome is a labyrinth through which the protagonist wanders — a man who has lost his spirituality and is searching for it anew. Sorrentino's film is not about beauty: it is about the search for meaning in a world where everything has turned into decoration and pretense. Rome here is not a city but the protagonist's inner state: his search, his return to the origins where meaning was still alive. And only by rediscovering that meaning does he once again become capable of writing a novel.
This website is built the same way — as a labyrinth in which to search for fundamental meanings →

    About

    About this exhibition

    This site is a guided wander through La Grande Bellezza (Paolo Sorrentino, 2013), the Academy Award–winning portrait of Rome as a city of dazzling surfaces and the search for meaning beneath them.

    It gathers thirteen real Roman locations tied to the film — all within one region and walkable in a single day — and presents each as a small exhibition panel: an identifying image, the exact shot position and camera orientation, systematic metadata, and texts you can adapt to your own depth and audience.

    The whole is built as a labyrinth, the film's own central image. From the home page you break the glittering surface of the city; beneath it lie the passages. Follow them in order along the one-day itinerary, or wander by theme, by kind of place, or by century.

    A student project for the University of Bologna, made by Maria Grigoryeva, Elnara Gasanova and Ekaterina Petukhova — see the page.

    Documentation

    How the project is built — and why

    Every choice below is deliberate; this page sets out the reasoning rather than presenting the design as arbitrary taste.

    One source of truth

    All content lives in a single data file (data.js): the locations, their metadata, the texts, the narratives and the timeline. The pages, the map, the timeline and the side menu are all rendered from it. This guarantees that metadata stays uniform and systematic across every location, and makes the site an "active document" — a mashup of data, map and typography rather than hand-written pages.

    The locations

    Thirteen real sites, all in Rome (one region) and sequenced into a single walkable day. Each carries the same fields: the exact shot position (coordinates), the camera orientation, an identifying image, the scene and its timestamp, and a Dublin Core metadata block (dcterms:title, subject, spatial, temporal, type) chosen to suit each entity — exterior monuments, interiors, gardens, fountains.

    The multimedia grid of texts

    Each location offers nine texts on two switchable axes: depth (brief · medium · in depth) and audience (young · adult · scholar). We deliberately fused the "competence" and "tone" dimensions into a single audience axis, because in practice they correlate — the young reader wants an introductory, plain register; the scholar wants the advanced, specialist one. Two clear controls give the visitor real authorship over the reading without an unmanageable matrix.

    Switchable themes

    Four graphic/typographic themes — Notte dorata, Carta, Cinema and Affresco — change palette and type pairing across the whole site, and your choice is remembered between visits. Each is a distinct reading of the film: Roman night, exhibition print, black-and-white cinema, baroque fresco.

    Narratives & the timeline

    Three guided routes (the one-day walk, "Jep's sacred Rome", "Decadence & the trick") give ordered ways through the material; thematic and typological lenses let you follow a single thread; and a dedicated arranges the places across two thousand years — the required diachronic reading.

    Navigation

    Every page carries previous/next navigation, the theme switcher sits in the top bar on all pages, and each location page has buttons to change the text's depth and audience. The interactive map draws the one-day itinerary as an ordered route.
